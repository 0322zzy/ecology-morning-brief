# Persistent research modules

Use these modules to interpret candidates and organize the brief. The searchable terms and weights in `config/interests.yaml` are authoritative.

1. **Thermal & physiological ecology** — thermal limits, performance, exposure duration, injury accumulation, thermal dose, microclimate, behaviour, and physiological bottlenecks.
2. **Climate-change vulnerability** — exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity, resilience, and multi-stage risk.
3. **Conservation biology** — priorities, protected areas, connectivity, biodiversity conservation, and adaptive management.
4. **Population ecology & demography** — survival, reproduction, vital rates, viability, and how individual responses scale to populations.
5. **IPM and stage-structured models** — embryo, juvenile, and adult stages in integrated population, Leslie, Lefkovitch, or related models.
6. **Spatial population dynamics** — dispersal, source-sink dynamics, connectivity, metapopulations, and spatial demography.
7. **Individual-based simulation** — population consequences emerging from individual behaviour, energetics, exposure, or movement.
8. **Ecological digital twins** — updateable virtual representations of ecosystems, populations, or biodiversity systems. Engineering twins may qualify in the methods lane when a concrete inference or updating technique transfers; do not include homonyms alone.
9. **Data assimilation & forecasting** — repeated observation-model updating, state-space inference, particle filters, ensembles, and ecological forecasts.
10. **Modern statistical ecology** — hierarchical Bayesian models, JSDMs, INLA, causal inference, latent processes, and multi-source integration.
11. **AI / machine-learning ecology** — foundation models, remote sensing, deep learning, graph neural networks, and biodiversity applications. Prefer papers that answer an ecological question or validate an ecological workflow.
12. **Eco-evolutionary responses** — plasticity, acclimation, population variation, local adaptation, rapid evolution, evolutionary rescue, and genomic vulnerability.

## Cross-cutting priorities

Give extra editorial weight to papers that connect two or more modules, especially:

- thermal tolerance across multiple life stages;
- exposure duration or thermal dose linked to survival;
- plasticity or local adaptation linked to climate vulnerability;
- physiological responses translated into vital rates or population viability;
- stage-structured, spatial, or individual-based models constrained by empirical thermal data;
- forecasting or digital-twin workflows that assimilate new ecological observations.

Generic method papers may still be useful, but they should not displace biologically grounded papers merely because they use fashionable terminology.

## Broad discovery modules

Also browse community assembly, ecosystem function and species interactions; climate extremes, carbon cycles, tipping points and Earth systems; and general causal/simulation-based inference, uncertainty, experimental design, active learning and scientific AI. CNS journal browsing is a separate discovery channel, independent of all topical modules. Weak direct project relevance is acceptable under the current persistent user preference.
